INTERNET OUTAGE — 19 OCTOBER 2026

Summary
The evidence supports a shared Internet-path interruption lasting approximately 12.5 minutes, with recovery by about 09:54 EDT and no operator modem power cycle. Cloudflare, Google and Quad9 public-IP pings failed together while the local pfSense gateway remained reachable. Traffic levels do not support local WAN saturation as the explanation. The available telemetry cannot locate the fault precisely within the modem, coax access network or upstream ISP path.

Scope and operator observations
Dashboard window: 13:33:54.858–14:40:38.520 UTC, or 09:33:54.858–10:40:38.520 EDT (America/New_York, UTC−04:00). Raw evidence includes five extra minutes of lead-in for counter calculations. The operator reported approximately 09:42–09:54, no modem power cycle, and a separate pfSense Unbound issue prolonging some service failures. Internet recovery is defined by public-IP ping recovery, not the later HTTPS recovery. Unbound causation is operator-reported; no resolver logs were collected.

This later application/DNS issue is excluded from the Internet outage duration.

WAN, firewall and local baseline
The pfSense LAN target records 0% one-minute loss throughout. During the requested pre-outage baseline, interval-average WAN download peaks at 0.957 Mbps and upload at 0.368 Mbps. During the core outage (09:42:00–09:53:30 interval endpoints), download is at most 0.00214 Mbps and upload 0.277 Mbps. Per-core CPU load is at most 4% in that core period. No sampled WAN errors or discards increase; ix2 stays operationally up and reports 1000 Mbps link speed. This is Ethernet link status, not proof of DOCSIS connectivity.
The largest download interval in the requested window is 210.576 Mbps ending 09:54:49, with 3.590 Mbps upload: it follows recovery, rather than preceding the outage. Values are approximately one-minute counter-delta averages and cannot exclude very short bursts or congestion elsewhere in the provider network. pfSense SNMP agent uptime increases without resetting; it is not a direct firewall boot-time measurement.

What the new modem data adds
The Hitron CODA5610Q remains in Bridge mode on firmware 7.2.4.5.3b7. Reported LAN and WAN uptime both increase from 63,551 seconds at 09:40:29 to 64,391 seconds at 09:54:29: exactly 840 seconds across 14 minutes, with no observed reset. That is consistent with the operator's report of no power cycle; interface-uptime reset semantics are not independently established, so it cannot rule out every internal modem or DOCSIS recovery action.
The modem API is available through most of the outage, with the single failed poll above. Exporter scrapes remain successful, the login counter stays at one, and WAN packet counters do not reset. Between 09:44:29 and 09:53:29, modem receive packets increase by only 787 (about 1.46 packets/s), while transmit packets increase by 90,034 (about 166.73 packets/s). Modem receive traffic rises sharply again around recovery, consistent with pfSense traffic and public ping observations. These counters' exact coverage in bridge mode remains unverified; transmit counts do not prove successful delivery through the ISP.
The brief modem API failure is correlated evidence of a local management interruption, not proof that the modem caused the Internet outage. DOCSIS power, SNR/MER, channel registration, codeword errors and cable event logs are not available in this dataset.

Collection integrity and comparison with October 18
All saved scrape-health samples are successful. Ping validity stays one, sending continues at one request per second, local ping send/receive error counters remain zero, and the ping process start timestamp is unchanged. There is no evidence here of the previous stuck-ping recovery problem or the Alloy recording-rule ingestion race. Unlike October 18, public-IP pings recover without a prober restart or operator modem power cycle. The 30-second recording-rule offset delays publication; it does not shift the stored measurement timestamps forward.

Conclusion and ISP follow-up
This is strong evidence of an interruption beyond the monitored local gateway path, affecting multiple independent public destinations at low local WAN utilization. It is not evidence of a single website failure or sustained local saturation. The precise failure location remains unproven. Ask the ISP to correlate 13:41–13:54 UTC on October 19 with modem registration/CMTS events, ranging timeouts, RF levels, codeword errors and neighborhood/upstream incidents. Keep the later operator-reported Unbound incident separate from this interval.
